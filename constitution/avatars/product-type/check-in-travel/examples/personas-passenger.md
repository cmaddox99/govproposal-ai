# Check-In Personas — Passenger Detail
# Companion to personas.md | Laws: PRD-2.3, PRD-1.1

---

## Persona 1: Alex — Digital Traveller

**Role:** Corporate executive, 15+ flights/year | **Age:** 38 | **Device:** iPhone

**Goals:** Zero friction — board without talking to an agent. Airport arrival to gate in 15 minutes.

**Pain Points:**
- Mobile check-in fails at gate 8% of the time → forced counter visit
- App logs out between sessions → re-authenticate at airport
- Boarding announcements don't tell him his zone or when to stand

**Key Behaviour:** Takes a screenshot of his boarding pass. *"I don't trust the app will load at the gate."* This is a product failure signal, not a user workaround.

**JTBD:** *"When I need to get somewhere, I want to board my flight without ever interacting with a person — gate to seat in under five minutes."*

**Success looks like:** Scans phone, hears beep, walks through. No conversation, no paper.

## Persona 2: Maria — Airport Traveller

**Role:** Occasional leisure traveller, visiting grandkids, 2-4 flights/year | **Age:** 68 | **Device:** Basic smartphone she rarely uses for apps

**Goals:** Get on the correct flight without confusion. Understand what's happening. Know who to ask if something goes wrong.

**Pain Points:**
- App buttons too small; can't read the seat map
- Doesn't know how to screenshot boarding pass
- Confused by boarding zones — unsure when to stand
- Worried about baggage: *"Will it make it? Where do I pick it up?"*
- Technology failures cause panic: *"The app crashed — what do I do?"*

**Key Behaviour:** Arrives 3 hours early. Calls airline before departure to confirm everything. Prints boarding pass "just in case." Asks gate agent for confirmation even after PA announcement.

**Quote:** *"I just want to make sure I get on the right flight to see my grandkids. If something goes wrong, I need someone to help me."*

**JTBD:** *"When I travel to visit family, I want to get through check-in without feeling lost or rushed, so I can focus on the trip rather than the process."*

**Success looks like:** Maria knows exactly where to go, what to do, and who to ask — with or without technology.

**Design implication:** Maria's 3-hour early arrival is a product failure signal. A clear, supported process would get her through in 45 minutes. The extra 2+ hours is anxiety tax we charge her.
